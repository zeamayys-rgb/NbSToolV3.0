NbS Tool is a free web tool for planning Nature-based Solutions in Southeast Asia. Give it a boundary and it reports what’s already there, what’s happening to it, and whether protecting, managing or restoring it fits. From there you can write the project up, plan the monitoring and keep the record as it runs. No GIS software needed.

The three key features.

NbS Tool is built around an Interactive Map, a Data Analyser and a Document Generator. Each one picks up where the last left off, so the boundary you draw is the one your documents are written about.

Interactive Map

Where you look around, and where you tell the tool which piece of ground you mean.

Explore the layers

Regional data layers for Southeast Asia, grouped as site information, nature, people and climate. Switch several on at once to compare them, and open any layer’s source and caveats from the legend.

Export the map

Take what you see into a meeting, a report or your own GIS. Export the map as an image at any time, with the layers you have switched on. Once you have selected an area, you can also export it as a GeoPDF that keeps its location information, or as KML for QGIS, ArcGIS and other mapping software.

Data Analyser

Clips every layer to your boundary and reports back. No GIS software or spatial-analysis background needed.

What’s there, and what threatens it

The site is summarised as general context, nature, people and climate, with the source and year behind each figure. A threat profile then shows how much of each ecosystem (forest, mangrove or peatland) is disturbed, what’s driving it, and the risk of flood, fire, landslide and drought.

Which pathway fits, and what it could deliver

Rules match each part of the site to Protect, Manage or Restore and state the reason. You choose the activities, and the tool estimates what they could deliver for nature, people and climate. These are screening figures for planning. They don’t replace field verification.

Document Generator

Turns a saved analysis into a document you can put in front of a funder.

Two templates to start from

A Feasibility Document, with the maps, baselines, benefit assessment and socio-economic profile a feasibility study starts from. Or a project document laid out to the Climate, Community & Biodiversity (CCB) Standards, which can be paired with Verra or Gold Standard.

Drafted from your analysis

Sections the analysis can support are filled in and marked, and you can edit every one. The parts only your project knows, such as land tenure, consent and benefit sharing, are left for you. Download the result as an editable Word document. Documents are built from a saved project, which needs a free account.

Before anyone drew a boundary, we went and listened.

SCeNe Coalition teams went to these sites to sit with the people who look after the land, before any boundary was drawn. What they heard decides how each project is scoped in NbS Tool, what its project document says, and what is put forward to the NbS Incubator. One thing held everywhere: the communities agreed to a study, not to a project. Every rule that comes later goes back to them for a decision.
